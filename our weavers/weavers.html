The Artisans Behind the Loom

Our Weavers

Meet the skilled hands that transform thread into timeless tradition.

Guardians of Heritage

Each weaver at Eravathody carries forward a legacy that spans generations. They are not just craftspeople; they are storytellers, preserving centuries of knowledge through their skilled hands and patient dedication. Their work sustains families, honors tradition, and keeps the art of handloom weaving alive.

Featured Artisans

At the loom since 1978

Ramakrishnan Nair

Master Weaver

Ramakrishnan began learning the craft at the age of twelve, sitting beside his father at the loom in their family home. Over five decades, he has mastered every aspect of handloom weaving — from setting up intricate warp patterns to executing the finest kasavu borders. His hands know the rhythm of the loom so well that he can weave in near darkness, guided only by muscle memory and lifelong practice.

"Weaving is not work," he says. "It is meditation. Every throw of the shuttle is a prayer."

The next generation

Lakshmi Devi

Weaver & Trainer

Lakshmi is one of the few women weavers at Eravathody, and she has carved a unique path in a traditionally male-dominated field. After joining the co-operative fifteen years ago, she quickly gained recognition for her precision and eye for color. Today, she not only weaves but also trains younger apprentices, passing on the techniques she has perfected.

"I want young people to see that this craft is not dying — it is evolving. We honor the old ways, but we also adapt."

Crafting with care

Suresh Kumar

Traditional Weaver

Suresh comes from a long line of weavers — his grandfather was one of the founding members of the Eravathody co-operative. For him, weaving is more than a livelihood; it is a connection to his ancestors. He specializes in traditional mundu and set sarees, maintaining the exact specifications and designs that have been used for over a century.

"When I sit at the loom, I feel my grandfather's presence. This is Deiva Thozhil — sacred work. We do it with reverence."

Learning the craft

Arun Mohan

Apprentice Weaver

At twenty-five, Arun represents the new generation of weavers at Eravathody. After completing his degree in commerce, he chose to return to his roots and learn the craft from his uncles. While his peers pursued corporate jobs, Arun found fulfillment in the quiet, deliberate rhythm of the loom.

"I see this as preserving something valuable. In a world of mass production, handmade things have meaning. I want to be part of that."

A Community of Craftspeople

Beyond individual artisans, Eravathody functions as a close-knit community. Weavers support one another, share knowledge, and collectively uphold the standards of their craft. The co-operative model ensures fair wages, mutual respect, and a shared sense of purpose.

Festivals, cultural events, and training programs bring the weavers together, reinforcing the bonds that have sustained this tradition for over eighty years. It is this sense of community — as much as skill — that keeps Eravathody thriving.
