Our Story

A journey through generations of handloom weaving in the heart of Kerala.

The Arrival of Mudaliyar

A few centuries ago, during a time of flourishing art and craftsmanship, the Raja of Kochi sought to elevate his court's prestige by commissioning garments of unmatched quality. To achieve this, he invited skilled weaving families from Tamil Nadu — renowned for their mastery in fine handloom techniques — to settle in his kingdom and create textiles exclusively for the royal family.

These artisans were warmly welcomed and granted land near the Eravath Mana. Over time, many of these families chose to remain in Kerala rather than return to Tamil Nadu, and the area came to be known as Eravathody. Thus emerged a unique weaving village in Kerala — where Tamil handloom traditions blended with the local ethos, creating a legacy of craftsmanship that continues to thrive.

Our Journey Through Time

1941

Society Formed

The Eravathody Handloom Weavers' Co-operative Society was established on 5th July 1941, bringing together 500 skilled artisans from the Mudaliar community.

1980

Relocation

The Eravathody Society relocated to its current structured facility, providing dedicated spaces for pit looms, frame looms, and storage.

2002

State Recognition

Inclusion in the Kerala Government's Handloom School Uniform Scheme and honored with the 'Kalaparam Aaya Kaithari' award for a Theyyam-inspired Kasavu design.

2010

No.1 Society

Recognized as Kerala's No.1 Handloom Society and awarded by the Directorate of Handloom & Textiles for outstanding contribution to the craft.

2015

Product Diversification

Expanded the product range beyond the traditional Kasavu saree to include dhotis, towels, and export-quality yardage, responding to evolving market demands while preserving authentic weave structures.

2025

Present Day

Continuing the legacy with 46 dedicated members, preserving Deiva Thozhil — the sacred duty of weaving.

Our Purpose

Vision & Mission

Our Vision

To be the enduring heartbeat of Kerala's handloom legacy, where the sacred duty of weaving — Deiva Thozhil — is celebrated and carried forward by future generations, ensuring the art of the loom never fades.

Our Mission

To preserve authentic handloom craftsmanship through collective effort, uphold the cultural integrity of the Kasavu tradition, and support our community of artisans with sustainable livelihoods and profound respect.
